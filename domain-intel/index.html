What it solves

Checking a domain properly means five tools: a WHOIS lookup, a DNS client, an SPF checker that counts nested includes, a DMARC parser and a TLS probe, followed by reading raw TXT records and deciding what matters.

Domain Intel does all of it in one call and returns one record per domain with an A to F grade, a one-line summary and a list of findings, each with a severity and a fix. It accepts domains, URLs or e-mail addresses and reduces them to the registrable domain.

  • Registration from official RDAP (found through IANA's bootstrap registry), never WHOIS scraping: registrar, dates, days to expiry, EPP status.
  • SPF DNS lookups counted recursively through every nested include and redirect, void lookups too, the way receiving servers count them.
  • DMARC policy and alignment, MTA-STS policy file, TLS-RPT, BIMI and DKIM key size per selector.
  • TLS certificate on the apex and www: trust, issuer, days left, hostname match.
  • Unknown domains, typos and failures cost nothing.

Limits

  • 5,000 domains per run. The registrable domain is analyzed: blog.example.com is checked as example.com.
  • DNS through public validating resolvers (Cloudflare and Google); 60 s per domain by default.
  • TLDs without RDAP show rdap: "unavailable"; WHOIS is never used.
  • TLS is checked on port 443 of the apex and www only. SMTP STARTTLS, DANE, blocklists and subdomain discovery are not covered.
  • DKIM keys can't be listed from DNS; pass your selectors for a definite answer.

Privacy

Reads only public DNS, public registry (RDAP) data and the certificates the domain's own servers present. Registrant personal data is dropped. No LLM, no other third party. See also the privacy page.

FAQ

How is the SPF 10-lookup limit counted?

Every include, a, mx, ptr, exists and redirect counts, including those inside nested includes, as RFC 7208 says. More than 10 is a SPF_TOO_MANY_LOOKUPS finding; more than 2 void lookups is flagged too.

How is the grade calculated?

The score starts at 100; each finding subtracts points (critical 30, high 15, medium 7, low 2). A is 90 or more, B 80, C 70, D 60, otherwise F. Any critical finding caps the grade at D.

Is this a WHOIS lookup?

Yes, through RDAP, the structured successor to WHOIS that registries now run: registrar, creation, update and expiry dates, status and nameservers, found through IANA's bootstrap registry. TLDs without RDAP are marked unavailable.

Does it return registrant names or e-mails?

No. Personal contact data in RDAP responses is dropped and never appears in the output.

Why does my domain show no DKIM?

DKIM keys live under selector names that can't be listed. Without dkimSelectors a few common selectors are probed and only found keys are reported; pass yours for a definite answer.

Can I check a domain that doesn't send mail?

Yes. Domains with a null MX (MX 0 .) are not marked down for missing MTA-STS or TLS-RPT.
